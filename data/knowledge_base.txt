ANXIETY MANAGEMENT
Anxiety is a normal human emotion, but when it becomes overwhelming, these strategies can help:
- Deep breathing: Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4. Repeat 5 times.
- Grounding technique (5-4-3-2-1): Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.
- Progressive muscle relaxation: Tense and release each muscle group from toes to head.
- Journaling: Write your worries down to externalize them from your mind.
- Limit caffeine and alcohol as they can worsen anxiety symptoms.
- Regular exercise releases endorphins that naturally reduce anxiety.

DEPRESSION SUPPORT
Depression is more than sadness. It affects how you think, feel, and function daily.
- Maintain a routine: Wake up, eat, and sleep at consistent times.
- Behavioral activation: Do small enjoyable activities even when you don't feel like it.
- Social connection: Reach out to one trusted person each day, even briefly.
- Sunlight exposure: 20-30 minutes of natural light daily improves mood.
- Avoid isolation: Depression thrives in isolation. Small social steps matter.
- Self-compassion: Treat yourself with the kindness you would show a good friend.
- Physical activity: Even a 10-minute walk can improve mood temporarily.

STRESS MANAGEMENT
Chronic stress affects physical and mental health significantly.
- Time management: Break large tasks into smaller, manageable steps.
- Set boundaries: Learn to say no to protect your energy and time.
- Mindfulness meditation: Focus on present moment for 5-10 minutes daily.
- Physical activity: Reduces cortisol (stress hormone) naturally.
- Sleep hygiene: 7-9 hours of sleep is essential for stress recovery.
- Social support: Sharing problems with trusted friends reduces stress burden.
- Hobbies: Engage in activities that bring joy and relaxation.

SLEEP PROBLEMS
Poor sleep significantly impacts mental health and vice versa.
- Sleep schedule: Go to bed and wake at the same time every day, including weekends.
- Screen-free time: Avoid phones and screens 1 hour before bed (blue light disrupts melatonin).
- Bedroom environment: Keep it dark, cool (65-68°F), and quiet.
- Avoid caffeine after 2 PM as it can stay in your system for 6-8 hours.
- Relaxation routine: Reading, gentle stretching, or warm bath before bed.
- Avoid lying in bed awake: If you can't sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something calm.

SELF-CARE PRACTICES
Self-care is not selfish — it is essential for mental health maintenance.
- Physical: Regular exercise, nutritious eating, adequate sleep, medical checkups.
- Emotional: Journaling, therapy, expressing feelings, creative outlets.
- Social: Quality time with supportive people, setting healthy boundaries.
- Mental: Learning new things, limiting news consumption, mindfulness.
- Spiritual: Meditation, time in nature, gratitude practice, purpose-driven activities.

WHEN TO SEEK PROFESSIONAL HELP
Professional help is a sign of strength, not weakness. Seek help when:
- Symptoms last more than 2 weeks and interfere with daily life.
- You are having thoughts of hurting yourself or others.
- Alcohol or substance use is increasing to cope with emotions.
- Relationships, work, or school are significantly impacted.
- You feel hopeless about the future consistently.

CRISIS RESOURCES
If you are in crisis or having thoughts of suicide, please reach out immediately:
- National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988 (call or text, available 24/7)
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 (available 24/7)
- Emergency Services: 911
- SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7)
- International Association for Suicide Prevention: https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/

MINDFULNESS AND MEDITATION
Mindfulness is the practice of being fully present in the current moment.
- Start with just 5 minutes daily using apps like Headspace, Calm, or Insight Timer.
- Body scan: Slowly notice sensations from head to toe without judgment.
- Mindful breathing: Focus entirely on your breath for 1-2 minutes when stressed.
- Walking meditation: Pay attention to each step, breath, and sensation while walking.
- Regular practice rewires the brain to respond rather than react to stressors.

BUILDING RESILIENCE
Resilience is the ability to bounce back from adversity and grow through challenges.
- Growth mindset: View challenges as opportunities to learn and grow.
- Strong social connections: Maintain relationships with supportive, positive people.
- Purpose and meaning: Connect daily actions to larger values and goals.
- Self-efficacy: Build confidence through small daily achievements.
- Emotional regulation: Practice identifying and expressing emotions healthily.
- Acceptance: Some things are beyond our control — focus on what you can change.
